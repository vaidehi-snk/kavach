Kavach steps in while the scammer is still talking.

An Alexa+ skill for older adults. It listens to suspicious calls, checks what they've been told, asks the real family when someone claims to be them, and speaks up before any money moves.

Four ways Kavach protects, all by voice.

Listens to the call

Put a suspicious call on speaker and say "Alexa, listen to this call." Kavach scores the whole conversation as it unfolds and cuts in the moment it turns into a scam.

"Aaji, stop. This call is a scam. Hang up now."

Asks the real family

Voices can be copied by AI. When a caller claims to be her grandson, Kavach sends the real one a Yes / No question on his own phone and tells her the truth.

"That is not Rohan. He just told me he is not calling you."

Checks anything she's told

Describe a text, WhatsApp, email or caller in your own words. Kavach names the scam, says the one fact that breaks it, and what to do next.

"Electricity companies never ask you to call a personal mobile number."

Keeps the family close

Daily check-ins, alerts when something's wrong, and a weekly summary for the family: scams stopped, how she's been feeling, anything worth a call.

"Aai checked in every day this week. Kavach stopped 2 scam calls."

Kavach never contacts anyone without asking her first.

Protection shouldn't feel like surveillance. Every alert to family is a question she answers by voice or tap, built on MCP elicitation, so the assistant cannot skip it. Calls are scored as they happen and are not recorded.

One MCP server. Any assistant can use it.

Kavach is a self-hosted Model Context Protocol server (spec 2025-11-25, Streamable HTTP). Alexa+ connects to it the way it connects to any integration; the demo uses a real MCP client in the same role.

  • Consent through elicitation. The server asks the person directly before anyone is contacted.
  • Progress notifications. While Kavach waits for the real family member to answer, the assistant hears how long is left.
  • Structured results. Every verdict comes back as typed data, so an assistant can act on it, not just read it.
  • Hard spending cap. Bedrock calls stop at a fixed budget and fall back to the rules engine.
